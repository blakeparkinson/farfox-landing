A care package is one of the few things that can get through the distance in one piece. It's a box your partner can open, hold, and keep, and it says you were thinking about them long before the delivery showed up.

The trouble is that most care packages end up as a random pile of snacks. Here's how to build one that feels like it came from you, with ten themed ideas, what to write in the note, and how to get it there without it being stuck in customs for a month.

---

## How to build a care package that feels personal

Start with a theme, then fill five slots. You don't need all five every time, but the mix is what makes a box feel thought through.

1. **Something to eat.** A snack from your city they can't get where they live, their favorite candy, or the tea you always drink together on calls.
2. **Something to use.** Cozy socks, a lip balm, a mug, a notebook. Everyday objects become reminders of you every time they're used.
3. **Something to read.** A handwritten note, a printed photo, a few [open when letters](/blog/open-when-letters/).
4. **Something that smells like you.** A T-shirt or hoodie you've worn, or a spritz of your perfume on the letter.
5. **Something to do together.** A face mask for each of you, a puzzle, a card game, a movie night kit. Open it on a video call.

The fifth slot is the one most people skip, and it's the one that turns a gift into a shared evening.

## 10 long-distance care package ideas

### 1. The bad-week box

For when they're stressed, sad, or just worn down. Comfort snacks, tea or hot chocolate, fuzzy socks, a candle, and a note that says you don't need them to be okay. Add one small thing that always makes them laugh.

### 2. The exam or deadline box

Good for students and anyone in a crunch at work. Snacks that don't need a kitchen, a nice pen, sticky notes with encouragement written on them, earplugs, and instant coffee. If you're in college, our [college long-distance guide](/blog/long-distance-relationship-college/) has more ideas for surviving the semester apart.

### 3. The countdown-to-visit box

Pack one small wrapped item for each of the last days before you see each other, numbered so they open one a day. Include the plan for your first evening together. Make a free [reunion countdown](/reunion-countdown/) and put the link in the note so they can watch the days drop.

### 4. The holiday box

Seasonal treats, a small ornament or decoration, a holiday card, and something to open on the day itself on a video call. If you're spending the season apart, our guide to [spending the holidays apart](/blog/long-distance-relationship-holidays/) covers call planning and gift timing around family plans.

### 5. The birthday box

A few small wrapped gifts instead of one big one, so the unwrapping lasts. Add candles, a party hat, and a card with one sentence for every year you've known each other. Schedule a call so you can watch them open it.

### 6. The date-night-in-a-box

Everything for a date you'll both have at the same time: the same snacks, a movie or game, a face mask each, and a menu card for the evening. Send a matching box to yourself. Our [long-distance date ideas](/blog/long-distance-date-ideas/) have plenty of themes to build a box around.

### 7. The sick-day box

Tissues, throat lozenges, soup packets, a funny movie recommendation, and a note that promises a call as soon as they feel up to it. Keep it small and quick to send, because the moment matters more than the contents.

### 8. The new-home or new-job box

For a big change in their life. Something for the new desk or kitchen, a plant (or a fake one if customs are tricky), and a letter about how proud you are. Include a framed photo of the two of you for the new space.

### 9. The cold-weather box

For the dark months. Wool socks, hand warmers, a blanket, hot chocolate, and a mug. A matching mug for yourself gives you a small ritual to share on morning calls.

### 10. The deployment box

If your partner is in the military, check the rules for their address and what's allowed where they're stationed before you pack. Letters, photos, and familiar snacks tend to matter most. Our [military long-distance guide](/blog/military-long-distance-relationship/) has more on staying close through a deployment.

## What to write in the care package note

The note is the part they'll keep. It doesn't need to be long. Try three things:

- **Why you chose each item.** "The tea is the one we drank in Lisbon" is worth more than the tea.
- **One specific memory.** A moment from your last visit, or a small thing you miss about them.
- **Something to look forward to.** The next visit, the next call, or the next thing you'll do together.

If you're stuck, borrow a starting line from our [love letter prompts](/blog/love-letter-prompts-long-distance/).

## How to pack and ship a care package

A little planning keeps the box from arriving crushed, late, or stuck in customs.

- **Keep it light and flat.** Shipping costs are usually based on weight and box size, so a small, full box is cheaper than a big, half-empty one. Fill gaps with socks or tissue paper so nothing rattles.
- **Choose food carefully.** Pick shelf-stable snacks in their sealed original packaging. Skip anything that melts, spoils, or leaks.
- **Check the restricted-items list.** Carriers and destination countries restrict certain items, often including some foods, liquids, aerosols, and batteries. Look up both before you pack, especially for international parcels.
- **Fill out customs honestly.** For international boxes, list every item and its value accurately. Vague or wrong descriptions are an easy way to get a parcel held.
- **Use tracking.** Share the tracking number so your partner knows when to expect it. Half the fun is the anticipation.
- **Send it early.** Holiday mail and international parcels are slower. If it needs to arrive by a certain date, leave plenty of buffer, and ship to wherever they'll actually be on that day.

## When shipping isn't an option

Sometimes the cost, the customs rules, or the timing just don't work. You can still send something:

- **Order from near them.** A local bakery, florist, or grocery delivery service in their city can drop off the snacks while you write the note.
- **Send something they can print.** A [personalized map of your two cities](/personalized-long-distance-map/) downloads as a print-ready file, so they can print it at a shop near them.
- **Go digital.** A playlist, a short video message, and a letter they can only open on a date you choose. Our [long-distance gift guide](/blog/long-distance-relationship-gifts/) has more digital gift ideas that don't feel like an afterthought.
