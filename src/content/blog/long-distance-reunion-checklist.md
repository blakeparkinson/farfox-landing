You've agreed to visit. Now one of you is comparing flights while the other is saving restaurants, and neither of you has checked whether Friday means a full day together or an arrival at 11 p.m.

Start with a short planning call. Agree on the dates, what you can afford, and how you want to spend the time. You can copy the checklist below into a shared note and fill it out together before booking.

This guide focuses on reunion visits. If you haven't met in person, read our [first-meeting guide](/blog/meeting-long-distance-partner-first-time/) too, and arrange your own transport and a place to stay if you need space or decide to leave.

## Your long-distance reunion checklist

Copy these prompts into the note you both use. Put booking references and addresses in a private note, separate from any countdown you share.

### Before either of you books

- **Dates:** arrival ___; departure ___; time zone ___. Confirm time off and any work or study commitments during the visit.
- **Budget:** our combined spending limit is ___. We will each pay ___ and keep ___ available for changes or unexpected costs.
- **Travel:** the traveller is ___; pickup or onward transport is ___. Check the full journey, including the trip home.
- **Stay:** we will sleep at ___. Confirm the arrangement with anyone who shares the home before booking travel.

### Before you pack

- **Plans:** one thing I want to do is ___; one thing you want to do is ___. Leave a block of time open.
- **Expectations:** discuss sleep, time alone, physical affection, and any family plans. Either person can change their mind about physical contact.
- **Arrival:** our meeting point is ___; if the journey changes, we'll contact each other by ___. Decide how you'll get food after arriving.
- **Essentials:** check the documents your journey requires, chargers, and anything you need for the stay. Save travel details somewhere you can access without a connection.

### Before you head home

- **Departure:** confirm checkout, transport, and enough time for your journey. Pack before the last-minute rush.
- **First check-in:** send an arrival message; our next call will be ___. Allow for sleep and travel delays.
- **Next step:** our next visit is ___, or we will compare dates on ___. Choose a planning date if you can't commit to travel yet.

## Set a budget you can both say yes to

Count more than the ticket. Add accommodation, transport at both ends, food, and any paid activities before you decide the visit is affordable. Include the cost of taking time off if that affects either person's pay.

You don't have to split each receipt in half. One person might pay for travel while the other pays for groceries and the stay, but compare the totals: a flight and a week's food can cost different amounts. Agree on a split that leaves both of you comfortable saying, “That restaurant is more than I want to spend.”

Try this wording on your planning call:

> “I can put ___ toward this visit, including travel. Can we work out the whole budget before I book?”

If the numbers don't fit, change the plan before paying. A shorter stay, another weekend, or fewer paid outings may suit you better. Neither person needs to spend more than they can afford to prove they care.

## Ask what each person means by a good visit

Imagine you've pictured two quiet days on the couch, but your partner has arranged dinner with friends, a family lunch, and a day trip. You can both feel disappointed despite looking forward to seeing each other.

Ask each other to name one thing you want to do and one thing you'd like to keep off the schedule. Talk through obligations: a four-hour work shift affects the visit even if someone works from home. Agree on how you'll spend that time instead of expecting the visitor to wait beside a laptop.

Physical closeness deserves its own conversation. Discuss what feels comfortable, including whether you want to share a bed. Booking a trip doesn't commit either of you to affection or sex, and you can change your mind during the visit.

For a longer stay, mention household habits before they cause friction. You might need an hour alone after work, an earlier bedtime, or help with cooking. Tell your partner rather than hoping they'll notice.

## A sample weekend with room to rest

Use this as a starting point, then adapt it to your arrival time and energy. A late flight may leave you wanting breakfast and a nap more than a morning outing.

| Time | A possible plan |
| --- | --- |
| Friday arrival | Meet, get food, and settle in. Keep the evening free of reservations if travel timing is uncertain. |
| Saturday morning | Have breakfast and choose how to spend the day after you've slept. |
| Saturday afternoon | Do the outing you picked together, with room to change plans. |
| Saturday evening | Cook at home or go to dinner within the budget you agreed on. |
| Sunday | Leave time to pack and travel. Pick your next call before you part. |

You can spend part of a visit doing ordinary things: shopping for dinner, reading beside each other, or taking a walk through your partner's neighbourhood. You don't need to fill the time with bookings.

Keep a backup for a cancelled outing. Choose a film or a recipe you both like before the trip, so rain doesn't leave you scrolling for an hour while hungry.

## Make arrival day easy on each other

Confirm the meeting point in words. “Outside arrivals by the café” is more useful than “I'll find you,” especially in a station or airport with several exits. Have a backup way to get to your accommodation if the person collecting you gets delayed.

The traveller may arrive tired, and the person waiting may want to start talking at once. Agree that either of you can ask for food, a shower, or sleep before an outing. If you feel shy after time apart, say so; you can take the reunion at your own pace without performing an airport scene for a camera.

Ask before posting photos of your partner or sharing travel details. Keep accommodation addresses and booking information out of public posts.

## Give the goodbye a little planning time

Discuss departure before the final hour. Confirm transport and decide when you'll check in after getting home. If you want a longer conversation about the visit, choose a time when neither person is rushing through a station.

You may know the date of the next trip, or you may need to save and compare schedules. In that case, put a planning call on the calendar. You can say, “Let's look at October weekends on Sunday,” without promising a ticket you can't buy.

Our [post-visit blues guide](/blog/post-visit-blues-long-distance-relationship/) covers the return to separate routines. For the time between visits, pick something small you both want to keep doing, such as [a question on your next call](/blog/long-distance-relationship-questions/) or a photo from your day.
